Lab 025 — Synthetic spin configurations from a conditional VAE: tested against the Hamiltonian

A conditional variational autoencoder (cVAE, Lab 024) produces new configurations of a spin model at a requested temperature \(T\), or at a requested point \((g,T)\) of a phase diagram. A generated snapshot can look like a Monte Carlo snapshot and still violate the physics fixed by the Hamiltonian. This lab tests the synthetic data in two independent ways:

  1. with networks trained on real Monte Carlo data: a regressor that reads \(T\) from a snapshot and classifiers of the phase;
  2. with the Hamiltonian itself: energy, magnetisation, specific heat, susceptibility, Binder cumulant, the distributions \(P(e)\) and \(P(m)\) and the correlation function, computed from the snapshots.

Every test is run first on held-out real data (this fixes the baseline and the statistical noise), then, identically, on synthetic data at the same conditions. Before the physics we check that the generator is trained properly (training curves, active latent units, a classifier two-sample test, memorisation).

Models: the 2D Ising model (\(L=30\), Part A) and the frustrated \(J_1\)–\(J_2\) Ising model (\(L=32\), Part B), with the Monte Carlo data of Lab 018.

Data. Run Lab 018 and copy ising_L30.npz and j1j2_L32.npz into data/. Everything runs on the CPU.

0a. Recap: Hamiltonian, Boltzmann distribution, observables

A classical spin model assigns an energy \(H(\mathbf s)\) to every configuration \(\mathbf s=(s_1,\dots,s_N)\), \(s_i=\pm1\), on an \(L\times L\) lattice with periodic boundaries (\(N=L^2\)): \[ \text{Ising: } H=-J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}s_is_j,\qquad J_1\text{–}J_2:\ H=-J_1\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}s_is_j+J_2\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle}s_is_j , \] with \(\langle ij\rangle\) nearest neighbours and \(\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle\) diagonal neighbours; \(J=J_1=1\), \(k_B=1\). In thermal equilibrium at temperature \(T\) (inverse temperature \(\beta=1/T\)) a configuration occurs with the Boltzmann probability \[ p(\mathbf s)=\frac{e^{-\beta H(\mathbf s)}}{Z},\qquad Z=\sum_{\mathbf s}e^{-\beta H(\mathbf s)} . \] Low \(T\): low-energy (ordered) configurations dominate; high \(T\): all configurations become nearly equally likely (disorder). Monte Carlo produces samples from \(p(\mathbf s)\); a generator should produce samples from the same distribution. What the observables measure:

observable physical meaning
\(e=H/N\) energy per spin: counts satisfied and broken bonds (local structure)
\(m=\frac1N\sum_i s_i\) magnetisation: ferromagnetic order (large below \(T_c\), small above)
\(m_{\rm str}\) stripe order of the \(J_1\)–\(J_2\) model (spins alternating along one lattice direction)
\(c=N\,\mathrm{Var}(e)/T^2\) specific heat: energy fluctuations, peaks at a transition
\(\chi=N\,\mathrm{Var}(\lvert m\rvert)/T\) susceptibility: magnetisation fluctuations, peaks at a transition
\(U_4=1-\langle m^4\rangle/3\langle m^2\rangle^2\) Binder cumulant: shape of \(P(m)\); \(2/3\) for two sharp peaks (ordered), \(\to0\) for a Gaussian (disordered)
\(G(r)=\langle s_{\mathbf x}s_{\mathbf x+r}\rangle\) spin–spin correlation at distance \(r\): how far order extends

For the 2D Ising model the transition is exactly known (Onsager): \(T_c=2/\ln(1+\sqrt2)\approx2.269\).

0b. Recap: the conditional ELBO for spins

A configuration \(\mathbf s=(s_1,\dots,s_N)\), \(s_i=\pm1\), is stored as bits \(b_i=(s_i+1)/2\in\{0,1\}\). The condition is \(c\) (\(c=T\) for Ising, \(c=(g,T)\) for \(J_1\)–\(J_2\)). The cVAE has three parts:

  • prior \(p(\mathbf z)=\mathcal N(\mathbf 0,\mathbb 1)\) over a latent vector \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb R^{d}\),
  • decoder \(p_\theta(\mathbf s\mid\mathbf z,c)=\prod_{i=1}^N \sigma(\ell_i)^{b_i}\,\big(1-\sigma(\ell_i)\big)^{1-b_i}\), with one logit \(\ell_i(\mathbf z,c)\) per site and \(\sigma(\ell)=1/(1+e^{-\ell})\),
  • encoder \(q_\phi(\mathbf z\mid\mathbf s,c)=\mathcal N\big(\boldsymbol\mu(\mathbf s,c),\mathrm{diag}\,\boldsymbol\sigma^2(\mathbf s,c)\big)\).

For every training configuration (Lab 024 derives this bound in full) \[ \log p_\theta(\mathbf s\mid c)\;\ge\;\mathbb E_{q_\phi(\mathbf z|\mathbf s,c)}\big[\log p_\theta(\mathbf s\mid\mathbf z,c)\big]\;-\; \mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(\mathbf z\mid\mathbf s,c)\,\|\,p(\mathbf z)\big)\;=\;\mathrm{ELBO}, \] \[ \log p_\theta(\mathbf s\mid\mathbf z,c)=\sum_{i=1}^N\Big[b_i\log\sigma(\ell_i)+(1-b_i)\log\big(1-\sigma(\ell_i)\big)\Big],\qquad \mathrm{KL}=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big(\sigma_j^2+\mu_j^2-1-\log\sigma_j^2\big). \] Here \(p(\mathbf z)=\mathcal N(0,\mathbb 1)\); in A.4 we also use a prior that depends on the condition, \(p_\psi(\mathbf z\mid c)\), and the KL term is then taken with respect to it. We minimise \(-\mathrm{ELBO}\) averaged over the data, with \(\mathbf z=\boldsymbol\mu+\boldsymbol\sigma\odot\boldsymbol\epsilon\), \(\boldsymbol\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\mathbb 1)\) (reparametrisation). Generation at a condition \(c\): draw \(\mathbf z\) from the prior, compute the logits, draw every spin from its Bernoulli distribution. Alternatively take the most likely configuration \(\hat s_i=\mathrm{sign}\,\ell_i\).

The physics behind the tests. The generator defines a model distribution \(p_\theta(\mathbf s\mid c)=\int d\mathbf z\,p(\mathbf z)\, p_\theta(\mathbf s\mid\mathbf z,c)\) that should approximate the Boltzmann distribution \(p(\mathbf s)=e^{-\beta H(\mathbf s)}/Z\). At fixed \(\mathbf z\) the decoder is a product over sites, the same structure as a mean-field ansatz: \(\langle s_i\rangle_{\mathbf z}=\tanh(\ell_i/2)\) and \[ \langle s_is_j\rangle_{\mathbf z}=\tanh(\ell_i/2)\tanh(\ell_j/2)\quad (i\neq j),\qquad \mathrm{Cov}(s_i,s_j)=\mathrm{Cov}_{\mathbf z}\big(\tanh(\ell_i/2),\tanh(\ell_j/2)\big). \] All correlations between spins must be carried by the few latent variables \(\mathbf z\). The energy \(H=-J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}s_is_j\) is a sum of nearest-neighbour correlations; a site-independent Bernoulli draw breaks bonds at random. This is why the energy, the specific heat and \(G(r)\) are the sharp tests here.

A.1 Data and split

ising_L30.npz holds 256 Swendsen–Wang configurations at each of 37 temperatures, \(T\in[1.5,3.5]\), denser near \(T_c\). Per temperature we split them into

  • S_train (128): training set of the generator and of the evaluator networks,
  • S_A, S_B (64 each): two independent held-out real sets. S_A is the reference for every test; the difference between S_A and S_B measures the statistical noise of a 64-snapshot estimate (real-vs-real baseline).

Array shapes: (n_T, n, L, L) = (temperatures, snapshots, rows, columns).

A.2 Observables fixed by the Hamiltonian

For one snapshot, with each nearest-neighbour bond counted once (right and down neighbour, periodic boundaries), \[ e=\frac{H}{N}=-\frac1N\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}s_is_j\in[-2,2],\qquad m=\frac1N\sum_i s_i . \] For a set of snapshots at one temperature (\(k_B=J=1\)): \[ \langle e\rangle,\quad \langle|m|\rangle,\quad c=\frac{N}{T^2}\big(\langle e^2\rangle-\langle e\rangle^2\big),\quad \chi=\frac{N}{T}\big(\langle m^2\rangle-\langle|m|\rangle^2\big),\quad U_4=1-\frac{\langle m^4\rangle}{3\langle m^2\rangle^2}, \] and the correlation function along the lattice axes, \(G(r)=\langle s_{\mathbf x}\,s_{\mathbf x+r\hat{\mathbf e}}\rangle\) (averaged over \(\mathbf x\), both axes and snapshots), \(r=0,\dots,L/2\).

ising_observables(S, T) takes S of shape (n_T, n, L, L) and T of shape (n_T,) and returns per-temperature averages (shape (n_T,)), the per-snapshot \(e\) and \(m\) (shape (n_T, n)) and \(G\) (shape (n_T, L/2+1)).

Code 
def ising_observables(S, T):
    '''Observables of the 2D Ising model (J = 1) for snapshots S (n_T, n, L, L) at temperatures T (n_T,).'''
    s = S.astype(np.float32)
    n_sites = s.shape[-1] * s.shape[-2]
    # each bond once: right neighbour (axis -1) and down neighbour (axis -2); np.roll implements periodic boundaries
    e = -(s * np.roll(s, 1, axis=-1) + s * np.roll(s, 1, axis=-2)).mean(axis=(-1, -2))     # (n_T, n)
    m = s.mean(axis=(-1, -2))                                                              # (n_T, n)
    T = np.asarray(T, np.float64)
    obs = {"e": e.mean(1),
           "m": np.abs(m).mean(1),
           "c": n_sites * e.var(1) / T**2,
           "chi": n_sites * ((m**2).mean(1) - np.abs(m).mean(1) ** 2) / T,
           "U4": 1.0 - (m**4).mean(1) / (3.0 * (m**2).mean(1) ** 2),
           "e_samples": e, "m_samples": m}
    # G(r): average of s_x s_{x+r} over sites, both axes and snapshots -> (n_T, L/2 + 1)
    obs["G"] = np.stack([0.5 * ((s * np.roll(s, r, -1)).mean((-1, -2, -3)) + (s * np.roll(s, r, -2)).mean((-1, -2, -3)))
                         for r in range(s.shape[-1] // 2 + 1)], axis=1)
    return obs

Distances between distributions. Averages can agree while the distributions differ. For two samples \(x_1..x_n\) and \(y_1..y_n\) of equal size, the 1D Wasserstein-1 distance is the mean absolute difference of the sorted values, \[ W_1=\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n\big|x_{(k)}-y_{(k)}\big| , \] i.e. the average distance one has to move probability mass to turn one histogram into the other (in units of \(e\) or \(m\)).

Noise units. For an observable \(O(T)\) we compare the synthetic estimate with S_A and normalise by the real-vs-real difference: \[ R_O=\frac{\mathrm{mean}_T\,|O_{\rm syn}(T)-O_A(T)|}{\mathrm{mean}_T\,|O_B(T)-O_A(T)|}. \] \(R_O\approx 1\): the synthetic set is as close to S_A as an independent Monte Carlo set of the same size. \(R_O\gg1\): a real discrepancy.

def wasserstein1(x, y):
    '''1D Wasserstein-1 distance between equal-size samples along the last axis: mean |sorted(x) - sorted(y)|.'''
    assert x.shape == y.shape
    return np.abs(np.sort(x, axis=-1) - np.sort(y, axis=-1)).mean(axis=-1)

def noise_ratio(O_syn, O_A, O_B):
    '''R = mean_T |O_syn - O_A| / mean_T |O_B - O_A|  (works for averages and for W1 distances: pass W1(syn,A), 0, W1(B,A)).'''
    return float(np.mean(np.abs(O_syn - O_A)) / np.mean(np.abs(O_B - O_A)))

A.3 Evaluator networks trained on real simulations

Two small CNNs, trained only on S_train (real data). Architecture: two \(3\times3\) convolutions with periodic (CIRCULAR) padding, a \(2\times2\) max pooling between them, global average pooling (so the network sees local patterns, not positions), one hidden dense layer.

  • temperature regressor, output \(\hat T\), loss \((\hat T-T)^2\), trained on all 37 temperatures;
  • phase classifier, trained only far from the transition: \(T\le1.8\) (ordered, label 0) and \(T\ge2.8\) (disordered, label 1); cross-entropy; \(p_1\) is the probability of “disordered” and \(\max(p_0,p_1)\) its confidence (Lecture 4).

One generic trainer is used for every network of the lab: one epoch is a single jit-compiled jax.lax.scan over minibatches, Adam with a cosine-decaying learning rate. The data arrays are passed as arguments (not captured as constants), spins stay int8 and are converted to float32 per minibatch.

Code 
def train_model(model, loss_fn, data, epochs, batch, lr, seed, callback=None, every=0):
    '''Minibatch training of an nnx model.
    loss_fn(model, batch_data, key) -> (scalar loss, aux vector);  data: tuple of arrays with the same leading dimension.
    Returns the per-epoch mean of aux, shape (epochs, len(aux)). callback(model, epoch) is called every `every` epochs and after the last one.'''
    n = len(data[0]); n_batches = n // batch
    schedule = optax.cosine_decay_schedule(lr, decay_steps=epochs * n_batches, alpha=0.05)
    opt = nnx.Optimizer(model, optax.adam(schedule), wrt=nnx.Param)
    graphdef, state = nnx.split((model, opt))              # pure pytree state -> usable inside jax.lax.scan
    data = tuple(jnp.asarray(a) for a in data)

    @jax.jit
    def run_epoch(state, data, key):
        k_perm, k_steps = jax.random.split(key)
        idx = jax.random.permutation(k_perm, n)[: n_batches * batch].reshape(n_batches, batch)   # (n_batches, batch)
        def step(state, inputs):
            batch_idx, k = inputs
            model, opt = nnx.merge(graphdef, state)
            (loss, aux), grads = nnx.value_and_grad(loss_fn, has_aux=True)(model, tuple(a[batch_idx] for a in data), k)
            opt.update(model, grads)
            return nnx.state((model, opt)), aux
        state, aux = jax.lax.scan(step, state, (idx, jax.random.split(k_steps, n_batches)))
        return state, aux.mean(axis=0)

    key, history = jax.random.key(seed), []
    for epoch in range(epochs):
        key, k = jax.random.split(key)
        state, aux = run_epoch(state, data, k)
        history.append(np.asarray(aux))
        if callback is not None and ((epoch + 1) % every == 0 or epoch + 1 == epochs):
            nnx.update((model, opt), state)
            callback(model, epoch + 1)
    nnx.update((model, opt), state)                        # write the trained parameters back into `model`
    return np.array(history)

class CNN(nnx.Module):
    '''(B, L, L) spins -> (B, n_out). Two 3x3 periodic convolutions, max pooling, global average pooling, dense head.'''
    def __init__(self, n_out, *, rngs):
        self.conv1 = nnx.Conv(1, 8, (3, 3), padding="CIRCULAR", rngs=rngs)
        self.conv2 = nnx.Conv(8, 16, (3, 3), padding="CIRCULAR", rngs=rngs)
        self.hidden = nnx.Linear(16, 32, rngs=rngs)
        self.out = nnx.Linear(32, n_out, rngs=rngs)
    def __call__(self, x):
        h = nnx.relu(self.conv1(x[..., None]))                           # (B, L, L, 8): add a channel axis
        h = nnx.max_pool(h, (2, 2), strides=(2, 2))                      # (B, L/2, L/2, 8)
        h = nnx.relu(self.conv2(h)).mean(axis=(1, 2))                    # (B, 16): global average pooling
        return self.out(nnx.relu(self.hidden(h)))

@nnx.jit
def cnn_forward(model, x):
    return model(x.astype(jnp.float32))

def predict(model, S, batch=4096):
    '''Apply a CNN to snapshots S (..., L, L); returns (..., n_out).'''
    flat = S.reshape(-1, *S.shape[-2:])
    out = np.concatenate([np.asarray(cnn_forward(model, jnp.asarray(flat[i:i + batch]))) for i in range(0, len(flat), batch)])
    return out.reshape(*S.shape[:-2], -1)

def mse_loss(model, batch, key):
    x, y = batch
    loss = jnp.mean((model(x.astype(jnp.float32))[:, 0] - y) ** 2)
    return loss, jnp.stack([loss])

def xent_loss(model, batch, key):
    x, y = batch
    logits = model(x.astype(jnp.float32))
    loss = optax.softmax_cross_entropy_with_integer_labels(logits, y).mean()
    return loss, jnp.stack([loss, (logits.argmax(-1) == y).mean()])

A.4 The conditional VAE

Dense encoder and decoder (the spins are flattened, x has shape (B, N), the condition c has shape (B, 1) and is \(c=T-2.5\)): \[ \text{encoder: } [\mathbf s, c]\;\xrightarrow{\;N+1\to512\to256\;}\;(\boldsymbol\mu,\log\boldsymbol\sigma^2)\in\mathbb R^{d}\times\mathbb R^{d},\qquad \text{decoder: } [\mathbf z, c]\;\xrightarrow{\;d+1\to256\to512\to N\;}\;\boldsymbol\ell , \] ReLU activations, latent dimension \(d=16\). We train three variants; each adds one physics-motivated ingredient to the previous one.

  1. cVAE: the model of Lab 024, prior \(p(\mathbf z)=\mathcal N(0,\mathbb 1)\), raw data.
  2. cVAE+Z2: the Ising Hamiltonian is invariant under the global flip \(\mathbf s\to-\mathbf s\). Below \(T_c\) the data consist of two mirror-image families (\(m\approx\pm|m|\)) that the generator would have to place separately in the Gaussian latent space; prior samples that fall between them decode to mixtures. We impose the symmetry by construction: every training snapshot is replaced by its representative with \(m\ge0\), and every generated snapshot is flipped with probability 1/2, so that \(p(\mathbf s)=\tfrac12\big[p_+(\mathbf s)+p_+(-\mathbf s)\big]\) exactly.
  3. cVAE+Z2+p(z|T): a conditional prior \(p_\psi(\mathbf z\mid c)=\mathcal N\big(\boldsymbol\mu_\psi(c),\mathrm{diag}\,\boldsymbol\sigma^2_\psi(c)\big)\), computed by a small network \(c\xrightarrow{1\to64\to}(\boldsymbol\mu_\psi,\log\boldsymbol\sigma_\psi^2)\) (the prior network of Sohn, Lee and Yan). The encoder sees the configuration, and a configuration itself reveals its temperature (ordered or disordered, large or small domains). The encoded snapshots of different temperatures therefore occupy different regions of \(\mathbf z\)-space. With the fixed prior \(\mathcal N(0,\mathbb 1)\), generation at temperature \(T\) draws \(\mathbf z\) from all these regions and produces a mixture of temperatures; with \(p_\psi(\mathbf z|T)\) it draws from the region of \(T\). The KL term becomes \[ \mathrm{KL}\big(q\,\|\,p_\psi\big)=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^d\Big[\log\frac{\sigma_{\psi,j}^2}{\sigma_j^2}+\frac{\sigma_j^2+(\mu_j-\mu_{\psi,j})^2}{\sigma_{\psi,j}^2}-1\Big], \] which reduces to the formula of Section 0b for \(\boldsymbol\mu_\psi=0\), \(\boldsymbol\sigma_\psi=1\).
Code 
class CVAE(nnx.Module):
    '''Dense conditional VAE with a Bernoulli decoder: x (B, N) spins +-1, c (B, n_cond).
    conditional_prior=False: p(z) = N(0, 1);  True: p(z|c) = N(mu_psi(c), sigma_psi(c)^2) from a small prior network.'''
    def __init__(self, n_sites, n_cond, latent=16, hidden=512, conditional_prior=False, *, rngs):
        self.enc1 = nnx.Linear(n_sites + n_cond, hidden, rngs=rngs)
        self.enc2 = nnx.Linear(hidden, 256, rngs=rngs)
        self.enc_mu = nnx.Linear(256, latent, rngs=rngs)
        self.enc_logvar = nnx.Linear(256, latent, rngs=rngs)
        self.dec1 = nnx.Linear(latent + n_cond, 256, rngs=rngs)
        self.dec2 = nnx.Linear(256, hidden, rngs=rngs)
        self.dec3 = nnx.Linear(hidden, n_sites, rngs=rngs)
        self.conditional_prior, self.latent = conditional_prior, latent
        if conditional_prior:
            self.pri1 = nnx.Linear(n_cond, 64, rngs=rngs)
            self.pri_mu = nnx.Linear(64, latent, rngs=rngs)
            self.pri_logvar = nnx.Linear(64, latent, rngs=rngs)
    def encode(self, x, c):
        h = nnx.relu(self.enc2(nnx.relu(self.enc1(jnp.concatenate([x, c], axis=-1)))))
        return self.enc_mu(h), self.enc_logvar(h)                          # (B, d), (B, d)
    def decode(self, z, c):
        h = nnx.relu(self.dec2(nnx.relu(self.dec1(jnp.concatenate([z, c], axis=-1)))))
        return self.dec3(h)                                                # (B, N) logits l_i
    def prior(self, c):
        '''Mean and log-variance of p(z|c), each (B, d); zeros (standard normal) without a prior network.'''
        if not self.conditional_prior:
            zeros = jnp.zeros((c.shape[0], self.latent))
            return zeros, zeros
        h = nnx.tanh(self.pri1(c))
        return self.pri_mu(h), self.pri_logvar(h)

def gaussian_kl(mu_q, logvar_q, mu_p, logvar_p):
    '''KL( N(mu_q, e^logvar_q) || N(mu_p, e^logvar_p) ) per dimension (diagonal Gaussians).'''
    return 0.5 * (logvar_p - logvar_q + (jnp.exp(logvar_q) + (mu_q - mu_p) ** 2) / jnp.exp(logvar_p) - 1.0)

def cvae_terms(model, x, c, key):
    '''Per-configuration terms of -ELBO: reconstruction -log p(s|z,c), shape (B,), and KL per latent dimension, shape (B, d).'''
    mu, logvar = model.encode(x, c)
    z = mu + jnp.exp(0.5 * logvar) * jax.random.normal(key, mu.shape)     # reparametrisation: one z per configuration
    logits = model.decode(z, c)
    bits = (x + 1.0) / 2.0                                                 # s = +-1  ->  b = 1, 0
    rec = optax.sigmoid_binary_cross_entropy(logits, bits).sum(-1)        # -sum_i [b log s(l) + (1-b) log(1-s(l))]
    kl = gaussian_kl(mu, logvar, *model.prior(c))                         # closed form, per dimension
    return rec, kl

def cvae_loss(model, batch, key):
    x, c = batch
    rec, kl = cvae_terms(model, x.astype(jnp.float32), c, key)
    loss = (rec + kl.sum(-1)).mean()
    return loss, jnp.stack([loss, rec.mean(), kl.sum(-1).mean()])

@nnx.jit
def decoder_logits(model, z, c):
    return model.decode(z, c)

def generate(model, cond, n_per_cond, key, side, symmetric=False, batch=8192):
    '''n_per_cond configurations at every condition row of cond (K, n_cond); z ~ p(z|c).
    Returns two int8 arrays (K, n_per_cond, side, side): Bernoulli samples and most likely configurations (same z).'''
    c_all = np.repeat(np.asarray(cond, np.float32), n_per_cond, axis=0)          # (K * n, n_cond)
    bern, ml = [], []
    for i in range(0, len(c_all), batch):
        k_z, k_s, k_f = jax.random.split(jax.random.fold_in(key, i), 3)
        c = jnp.asarray(c_all[i:i + batch])
        mu_p, logvar_p = model.prior(c)                                          # (b, d) each; zeros for N(0, 1)
        z = mu_p + jnp.exp(0.5 * logvar_p) * jax.random.normal(k_z, mu_p.shape)  # z ~ p(z|c)
        logits = decoder_logits(model, z, c)                                     # (b, side*side)
        s_b = jnp.where(jax.random.bernoulli(k_s, jax.nn.sigmoid(logits)), 1, -1)   # independent Bernoulli spins
        s_m = jnp.where(logits > 0, 1, -1)                                        # most likely configuration
        if symmetric:                                                             # global flip with probability 1/2
            flip = jnp.where(jax.random.bernoulli(k_f, 0.5, (len(c), 1)), -1, 1)
            s_b, s_m = s_b * flip, s_m * flip
        bern.append(np.asarray(s_b, np.int8)); ml.append(np.asarray(s_m, np.int8))
    shape = (len(cond), n_per_cond, side, side)
    return np.concatenate(bern).reshape(shape), np.concatenate(ml).reshape(shape)

A.8 Generator quality: two-sample test and memorisation

Classifier two-sample test (C2ST). If two sets come from the same distribution, no classifier can tell them apart better than chance. At three temperatures we train a fresh small CNN to separate real from synthetic snapshots (balanced classes, 75% for training, 25% held-out) and report the held-out accuracy: 0.5 means indistinguishable, 1.0 means trivially separable. Sanity check first: real (S_train) against real (S_A+S_B) must give \(\approx0.5\).

Memorisation. A generator that copies its training set would pass every physics test. For every synthetic and every held-out real snapshot we compute the normalised Hamming distance to the nearest training snapshot at the same \(T\), minimised over the global flip: \[ d_{\rm NN}(\mathbf s)=\min_{\mathbf t\in\text{train}}\ \frac{1-|\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf t|/N}{2}\in[0,\tfrac12]. \] Real held-out snapshots are new draws from the Boltzmann distribution, so their \(d_{\rm NN}\) is the reference. Synthetic \(d_{\rm NN}\) much smaller than real means copying.

def c2st(X1, X2, seed, epochs=25):
    '''Held-out accuracy of a CNN trained to separate X1 (n1, L, L) from X2 (n2, L, L).
    Both classes are cut to n = min(n1, n2) and split 75 / 25 *within each class* (stratified): with a plain random split a
    class that is over-represented in training is under-represented in the test set, which biases the accuracy below 0.5.'''
    n = min(len(X1), len(X2)); n_tr = int(0.75 * n)
    r = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    p1, p2 = r.permutation(len(X1))[:n], r.permutation(len(X2))[:n]
    X_tr = np.concatenate([X1[p1[:n_tr]], X2[p2[:n_tr]]]); y_tr = np.repeat(np.array([0, 1], np.int32), n_tr)
    X_te = np.concatenate([X1[p1[n_tr:]], X2[p2[n_tr:]]]); y_te = np.repeat(np.array([0, 1], np.int32), n - n_tr)
    model = CNN(2, rngs=nnx.Rngs(seed))
    train_model(model, xent_loss, (X_tr, y_tr), epochs=epochs, batch=32, lr=3e-3, seed=seed)
    pred = predict(model, X_te).argmax(-1)
    return float((pred == y_te).mean()), len(y_te)

A.9 Reading Part A

What the tests show:

  • Training health is similar for the three models: the held-out \(-\)ELBO is close, all latent units are active, about 80% of the spins are reproduced from \(\mathbf z=\boldsymbol\mu\). The ELBO alone does not tell which model generates the right physics.
  • cVAE. Generation at a fixed \(T\) produces a mixture of temperatures: \(\langle|m|\rangle\) is far too small below \(T_c\) and too large above, the regressor reads temperatures that depend only weakly on the requested one, and below \(T_c\) a large fraction of the snapshots are mixtures of the two ordered states.
  • cVAE+Z2 removes part of the mixtures, but the temperature is still not followed.
  • cVAE+Z2+p(z|T) follows the temperature: \(\langle|m|\rangle\), \(\chi\), \(P(m)\) and the regressor below \(T_c\) are now close to the Monte Carlo values (within a few noise units). The local tests fail: \(\langle e\rangle\) is too high around and above \(T_c\) by tens of noise units, \(c\) is an order of magnitude too large, and \(P(e)\) is far too broad. Given \(\mathbf z\) the spins are drawn independently, and every spin whose logit is not saturated breaks its bonds at random.
  • Most likely configuration \(\hat s_i=\mathrm{sign}\,\ell_i\) removes that noise and over-smooths: \(\langle e\rangle\) too low, large compact domains, \(G(r)\) too large at all distances, a regressor that reads temperatures far too low.
  • The C2ST separates every synthetic set from Monte Carlo data, clearly so near and above \(T_c\); real against real gives 0.5. The generator does not memorise: the nearest-neighbour distances of the best model to the training set are those of new Monte Carlo snapshots.

Global, pattern-level quantities (\(\langle|m|\rangle\), the regressor, the classifier) are more forgiving than the local, Hamiltonian-level ones (\(\langle e\rangle\), \(c\), \(P(e)\)). A generator that passes only the first kind of test looks right.

Part B: the \(J_1\)–\(J_2\) model

\[ H=-J_1\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}s_is_j+J_2\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle}s_is_j,\qquad g=J_2/J_1,\quad J_1=1 , \] ferromagnet for \(g<1/2\), stripes for \(g>1/2\) (Lab 018). Per snapshot: \(|m|\), the stripe order parameter \(m_{\rm str}=\sqrt{m_{(\pi,0)}^2+m_{(0,\pi)}^2}\) with \(m_{(\pi,0)}=\frac1N\sum_{x,y}(-1)^x s_{x,y}\), the energy per spin \(e=-\frac1{2N}\sum_i s_i h_i\) with \(h_i=\sum_{\rm nn}s_j-g\sum_{\rm nnn}s_j\), and per grid point \(c=N\,\mathrm{Var}(e)/T^2\).

Protocol. The condition is \(c=(g,T)\). At every grid point the 100 snapshots are split into training (60), S_A (20) and S_B (20). The cVAE is trained only at every second \(g\) and every second \(T\) (a quarter of the grid, “seen”) and generates at every grid point, so three quarters of the conditions were never seen in training. The phase classifier is trained on real training snapshots at all points.

def j1j2_observables(S):
    '''Per-snapshot |m|, m_str, e and stripe orientation for S (nG, nT, n, L, L); outputs (nG, nT, n).'''
    phase_x = np.where(np.arange(LJ) % 2 == 0, 1.0, -1.0)[None, :]       # (1, L): (-1)^x along the column index
    phase_y = phase_x.T                                                   # (L, 1): (-1)^y along the row index
    out = {k: np.zeros(S.shape[:3], np.float32) for k in ["m", "m_str", "e", "orient"]}
    for a in range(S.shape[0]):
        s = S[a].astype(np.float32)                                       # (nT, n, L, L)
        nn = sum(np.roll(s, sh, ax) for sh in (1, -1) for ax in (-1, -2))                                 # 4 nearest neighbours
        nnn = sum(np.roll(np.roll(s, sy, -2), sx, -1) for sy in (1, -1) for sx in (1, -1))                # 4 diagonal neighbours
        out["e"][a] = -0.5 * (s * (nn - g_grid[a] * nnn)).mean((-1, -2))   # each bond appears twice in sum_i s_i h_i
        m_pi0, m_0pi = (s * phase_x).mean((-1, -2)), (s * phase_y).mean((-1, -2))
        out["m"][a] = np.abs(s.mean((-1, -2)))
        out["m_str"][a] = np.sqrt(m_pi0**2 + m_0pi**2)

B.1 Reading Part B

  • The phase classifier trained on real data assigns the synthetic snapshots to the same phase as the Monte Carlo snapshots at most grid points, at seen and at unseen \((g,T)\) alike (real-vs-real: almost 100%): the generator interpolates the phase diagram. The phase boundary of the synthetic data is smeared, and near \(g=1/2\) and close to the transition lines the two maps differ.
  • The order parameters \(\langle|m|\rangle\) and \(\langle m_{\rm str}\rangle\) are reproduced within a few noise units; the two stripe orientations are generated with equal frequency, as in the Monte Carlo data.
  • The energy fails as in the Ising case: in the paramagnet the synthetic \(\langle e\rangle\approx0\), the value for independent spins, while the Monte Carlo energy shows the short-range correlations of the paramagnet; the specific heat is wrong by orders of magnitude.
  • The most likely configurations are ordered almost everywhere: the classifier sees ferromagnets or stripes deep in the paramagnet.

Conclusions.

  • A cVAE trained on Monte Carlo data reproduces what the networks trained on real data look at: the phase, the order parameter and its distribution, the position of the transition; for \(J_1\)–\(J_2\) also at conditions it has never seen.
  • Two ingredients were needed, both physics: the \(\mathbb Z_2\) symmetry of \(H\) imposed by construction, and a prior that depends on the condition, so that \(\mathbf z\) is drawn from the region of latent space that belongs to the requested temperature.
  • It does not reproduce what the Hamiltonian measures locally: the energy, its fluctuations (the specific heat) and \(P(e)\). Given \(\mathbf z\) the decoder is a product over sites, a mean-field-like distribution, and nearest-neighbour correlations beyond what the 16 latent variables carry are lost. A classifier two-sample test detects this at every temperature.
  • Synthetic configurations can be useful for questions about the phase; they are not samples of \(e^{-\beta H}/Z\) and cannot replace Monte Carlo for thermodynamics.
